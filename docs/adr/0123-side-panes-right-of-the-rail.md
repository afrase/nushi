# 0123. Side panes right of the details rail, which tucks itself away

Status: accepted (the owner, 2026-11-15).

## Context
A chat was laid out transcript | side panes | details rail, the chat's header across all of it, and the side panes (the side chat, Files, a subagent's or a command's pane, the preview: a file or a browser tab) lay over the transcript once it got too narrow, the rail staying put. The owner, with screenshots of Claude's desktop app (`nushi-review/2026-11-15-right-panes/owner/`): "I want the browser and file viewer to open to the right of the widget rail like claude desktop. I also want it to auto-hide when it would make the chat too small." Claude's header ends at its rail's right edge and its browser's tabs are in the window's top row; on moving the header's buttons for that: "I think maybe everything should? If we move the buttons then we unlock more realastate for other things."

## Decision
- **Order: transcript | details rail | every side pane**, the panes in their old order among themselves (side chat, Files, subagent or command, preview).
- **The chat's header spans the transcript and the rail; each pane runs the pane's full height**, its own top row `PaneHeader.height` high, its hairline level with the header's. The browser's and the file preview's tabs were already in that row, which is now the window's top row (ADR 0070); each pane keeps its × and its buttons there, and its address bar or path line under it. The terminal runs under the transcript and the rail.
- **Over the transcript, the same layout.** Narrower than the beside line, where the panes (each at its least) lie over the transcript, they still run the pane's full height from its top with their own top rows. The chat (its header, transcript and terminal) keeps the transcript's least width and lies under them, covered at its right, the header's buttons with it; the header never spans the panes. Nothing changes place at the line: the panes' left edge and the header's width move a point for a point of window, at every line, and the first pane's shadow falls on the transcript.
- **The header's buttons move with the panes**: opening a pane puts its left edge where the buttons ended, and they glide left by its width, in step with its slide. This is a deliberate exception to "a toggle never moves when pressed" (ADR 0111): the opened pane takes their place. The details' and the terminal's buttons still never move them.
- **The rail tucks itself away for the panes' room, then the panes give width.** One pure function (`besideFit`, `beside_fit.dart`) shares the room. The rail goes first, when the transcript would keep less than 484 beside it (360 beside a subagent's pane or the preview). Then, where the transcript would still keep less than its least beside the panes alone (556 beside the side chat or Files, 360 beside those wide panes), the panes narrow toward their own least widths, live and never saved: the widest gives first (the preview or a subagent's pane, as a rule), down to its least, then the next, the transcript keeping its least. Their saved and dragged widths stay as the user left them, and are back as soon as there's room. Only once every pane is at its least do they lie over the transcript. The transcript's least widths are the old ones (ADR 0092): the new order changes what gives way first, not how narrow the transcript gets. With no pane the rail shows from a chat area of 804, as before.
  - It's decided on widths alone, the rail an input to nothing: whether the panes lie over is read from their least widths alone, the rail and the narrowing from the widths as dragged. A pane's edge dragged while the panes are beside the transcript stops where it keeps its least, the rest as they are (narrowed, a pane can be dragged narrower, which gives the rest their width back, but no wider), so a drag can't move the panes over it under the pointer; the rail tucks and comes back as the edge moves. Lying over it, a pane is at its least, and its edge stops there (an uncapped pane went on to 48 before). A pane being dragged gives width last. Widths are whole points while dragged.
  - It's never saved: `showDetails` and `app.json` keep the user's choice, the header's button is lit by it ("Hide details (tucked away while the panes need the room)"), a press changes only the choice, and Artifacts… lists in the palette while there's no room. Tucked, the rail stays built (not drawn, not reached, its tickers off), so it comes back as it was and asks the daemon nothing again; it goes on asking what fills the context after each turn (at most once a minute), as a shown one does, so the Context widget is current when it's back; the focus in it goes to the chat's keys. It slides as the panes do; Reduce Motion jumps.
- **Expand covers the whole chat pane** (ADR 0094), its header and terminal too; its tabs start past the traffic lights with the sidebar hidden. ⌃` while expanded puts it back and shows the terminal in one press (one already open under the cover is shown, not closed); the chat's terminal opening any other way puts it back too.
- **In a chat narrower than the panes' least widths, they narrow alike.** Over the transcript a pane is never narrower than its least, so in a chat area narrower than the open panes' least widths (the side chat, Files and the preview need 800) the leftmost would run past its left edge and be cut. They are narrowed instead to fit from its left edge (`Beside`), or with the sidebar hidden from past the title buttons (ADR 0118), which would otherwise lie over the first pane's top row, now in the window's top row: first each toward the least its rows hold (`PaneSpec.squeezed`: Files 200, whose rows fold and whose tree gives way to its viewer there; the side chat 240; the rest their least), all in step, and only past that each by the same share (`squeeze`, continuous, so no edge jumps); wider, they're back at their least or more. By the same share from the start, Files' least of 500 took the others below what their rows hold in windows up to about 780. Leaving the leftmost out was tried and dropped: Files would vanish as a file opened from it in a 720 window. Narrowed so, a pane's top row shortens its words, never its buttons: the side chat's and Files' captions are cut short, then go, then their names (`PaneTitle`); then Keep as chat is its icon alone, then the side chat's hide goes (its key stays); Files' top row drops Files, then the kind and Live, then the name, then Expand, and its second row folds its file's buttons, the source button too, into the ⋯, then Collapse and the tree's button (both in the ⋯).
- An agent's `show` opens the preview where it fits with the rail tucked and the panes narrowed to their least (ADR 0068's "never over the box" holds).
- The phone is untouched; Linux has the same layout.

## Consequences
- Between the old line where the panes lay over the transcript and the new one, they stay beside it, the rail tucked away, and narrow below the middle column (the table below; A is the chat's pane, the window less 268 with the sidebar, 16 without). Above the first column the rail shows; under the last, the panes lie over the transcript, each at its least (side chat 300, Files 200, subagent or command 220, preview 300).

  | Open (default widths) | Rail from A | Narrowing under A | Beside from A |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | none | 804 | — | — |
  | side chat 380 | 1184 | 936 | 856 |
  | Files 260 | 1064 | 816 | 756 |
  | side chat + Files | 1444 | 1196 | 1056 |
  | subagent or command 264 | 944 | 624 | 580 |
  | preview 560 | 1240 | 920 | 660 |
  | Files + preview | 1500 | 1180 | 860 |
  | side chat + Files + preview | 1880 | 1560 | 1160 |

- A header 360 wide happens now. Past the Jira key and the GitHub chips, which fold into ⋯, the agent's mark folds (the state's dot's tooltip names the agent), then the tabs' field, into ⋯ as the tabs, so the title keeps its 80 even with the sidebar hidden on a Mac, where the traffic lights take the row's start.
- A header with no pane open changed too, as the owner accepted: it gives its title the room the row leaves and measures the agent's name (f8650d40), where it had capped both from worst-case widths, so the header's row in the `spacing_turn_720`, `header_menu`, `reopen` and `reopen_gone` goldens moved.
- Over the transcript the chat's header and its box are covered at their right, the header's ⋯ and the box's send button with them, until the window widens or a pane closes.
- The notices still float centred on the whole pane, so they can lie over a pane's address row; centring them on the chat is a follow-up. (Done otherwise on 2026-11-17, [0128](0128-toasts-come-from-the-top.md): they take a row under the chat's header, as wide as it, and never lie over a pane.)
- The Changes and Pull request tabs have no panes, as before; the header and the terminal stay the same widgets from tab to tab.

## Alternatives considered
- Keeping the rail at the right with the panes left of it: not what the owner asked; Claude's rail is beside the chat.
- Panes lying over the transcript at their saved widths as soon as it would keep less than its least (this ADR as first accepted): between that line and the one where they're all at their least, a pane covered the transcript that could have given it room, and at the line the window's layout jumped.
- Narrowing the transcript's least beside every pane to 484: the rail would hide where it showed before beside a preview.
- Saving the hide (turning `showDetails` off): the panel would stay away once there's room again, and the user's choice would be the window's.
- Panes under the header at every width, as before: the header's buttons couldn't then end where Claude's do, and every pane lost a row's height.
- Over the transcript, the header and the terminal across the whole pane and the panes under the header (this ADR as first accepted): a point past the beside line the window switched layouts, the header's buttons jumping to the far right, the panes dropping under it, the transcript re-centred and cut mid-sentence.

## Amendment (2026-11-15): Files holds the open files, as Claude desktop's does

The owner, with Claude desktop's Files pane (`nushi-review/2026-11-15-right-panes/owner/19–22`): "We should copy claude code now that we open on the right for files. It shows a blank space for open files and it also keep the tree on the right instead of left."

- **One Files pane: [viewer | tree].** The file viewer (what the preview pane's file side was, `FileView`) is the Files pane's left part and the tree (`FilesTree`) its right part, at the window's edge, its own edge dragged (`filesTree`, 260, from 200 to 480; `files` stays the Changes list's width). With no file open the viewer is blank, as Claude's: a folder, "Open files appear here" and "Pick a file in the tree, or click a file path in the conversation" (Nushi's empty states have no full stops). Opening a file never moves the tree. Tab goes through the viewer and the tree each in turn, in the order they read on either side (each a focus group: the viewer, as tall as both, took the tree's rows before its search with the tree on the left). Without the tree (hidden, or squeezed out) the viewer is alone, and its blank says "Go to a file with ⌘P, or click a file path in the conversation" (amended 2026-11-24: a daemon older than the tree, `filesApi`, left the viewer alone too, and is now below the app's floor, ADR 0004).
- **The pane's two rows.** The top row, level with the chat's header: Files at its left; after it the shown file's kind and name with its ✕, or with two or more open their tabs (ADR 0070's plain tabs, after the name rather than in its place: the pane is Files, not one file), each led by its own file's kind where the one file's was (a single icon before them labelled only the first tab); Live; and at its right end ×, in the same boxes and at the same pixels as the browser's (`PaneEndButtons`). The second row (`FilesBar`, 40 high, across the whole pane): the shown file's path line over the viewer, the source button, a page's Reload and Open in browser, and Show in Finder, those buttons folded into its ⋯ (after a line, last, so Claude's rows keep their places) when the row is too narrow; at its right end Claude's three, below. The git-ignored eye is in the tree's filter field.
- **Files' × hides it and keeps its tabs**, as ⇧⌘E, View › Files and the header's button do: one meaning, and no question on a toggle; hiding saves an edited file as its editor goes (ADR 0096), and one in a conflict keeps its text in memory. ⌘W closes the shown file; the last leaves the viewer blank and the pane open.
- **Every way a file opens opens Files on it** (`ChatState.openInFiles`): a path in the conversation, a tool row's Preview, a message's file chip, the agent's `show`, ⌘P, Artifacts, the tree, and a `file:` address typed in the browser. Opened so, the pane is open for this run only (`filesPassing`): its tabs aren't kept once Nushi quits, so a pane kept would come back with none of them, 820 wide, tucking the rail, which the user never asked for; opened by its own button, ⇧⌘E or View › Files, it's kept as before. The file view's tabs are `FileTabs`, apart from the browser's (`BrowserTabs`): the browser is a pane of its own (ADR 0094), and a subagent's pane takes only its place.
- **Order: side chat, Files, subagent or command, browser.** Files is a wide pane (`PaneSpec.wide`): 820 at first (the viewer 560, the tree 260), 500 at least (300 and 200), no most. Inside it the viewer gives width first, to 300, then the tree, to 200 (`treeWidth`, `files_split.dart`); squeezed narrower still (lying over the transcript in a narrow window), the tree gives on, to nothing, while the viewer keeps 300 and then the whole pane, point by point, so no edge jumps; the same with a file open or none, so opening one never moves the tree, and once the tree has no width the blank viewer names ⌘P instead of it. `filesTree` isn't touched. Narrowing the two alike, as first built, left a 720 window's Files beside the side chat and the browser a viewer of 83 and a tree of 57, its file's name breaking a letter or two a line. Giving Files more of the squeezed room was tried too: from its 200 to the viewer's 300, or by the same share of its 500 as the rest, it takes the side chat's box and a subagent pane's tool rows below what they hold in windows under about 760. Hiding the tree when narrow was weighed and left out: a third thing tucking itself away would jump the viewer by 200 and undo the user's choice; the tree keeps its least.
- Between the panes the rule is the one above: the widest gives first, so with the browser open Files gives its 320 before the browser gives any.
- **Costs**: with Files open the rail tucks below a chat of 1500 (it was 1064 with Files alone), and an agent's `show` that has to open Files needs a chat of 860 (it was 660 for the preview); an open Files pane always takes it. Both follow from a viewer in Files; Files' opening width is the knob if they're too much. In the narrowest windows a squeezed pane drops what it can (the browser's Servers and ⋯, then its +, while its showing chip keeps 88, then a lone chip shrinks to its icon and ✕; its bar's Not secure, then Back and Forward, then Reload, their keys staying; the side chat's icon) before its buttons are cut.

## Amendment (2026-11-17): the sidebar tucks away in a narrow window, and the tree goes rather than leave a sliver

The roadmap's "Panes at narrow widths": with the sidebar shown in a narrow window the chat had what the sidebar left, 157 in a 400 window and 237 at 480 with no pane open, its rows running past their ends; a pane lying over it covered all of that. Files' tree, squeezed between its viewer's least and both's, was a sliver of 1 to 70 in windows of about 520 to 580, its field and rows running past it.

- **The sidebar tucks itself away**, as the details rail does for the panes: where the window would leave the main area beside it (less the window's margin) narrower than 360 (`ChatView.leastBesideWide`, the least the transcript is ever laid out at, beside the panes or under them), it isn't shown (`sidebarTucksIn`, `sidebarTucked`): a window under about 603, the sidebar at its least there. It's never saved (`sidebarHidden` keeps the user's choice) and is back as soon as the window has room. Tucked, it counts as hidden for the layout (`sidebarShut`: the title buttons, the headers' start) and its button says Show; the button, ⌘\, the palette and the menu bar float it over the view as a peek (ADR 0118) rather than change the choice, and ⌘\ again puts the peek away. It's decided on the window alone: the panes then share the room it leaves as above, so no window of 603 or more changes, and opening a pane never moves the sidebar.
- **The tree goes rather than leave a sliver**: squeezed, it gives on from 200 only to 120 (`filesTreeFolds`, what its search field and rows hold); narrower, it isn't there and the viewer has the whole pane. That's a jump of 120 at a Files pane of 420, the one place an edge in the pane jumps.
- **A tool row's header holds at any width**: what's after the title (its counts, a background command's state, failed) takes at most the row less the icon and 48 for the title, its words ending in an ellipsis, and the spinner and a show call's Open go before they'd run past, in any font.
- **An edge's drag ends once, whatever ends it** (amended 2026-11-19): released, cancelled, its press let go unseen, locked or gone mid-drag, `onDrag` hears null once, after the frame when the edge went with it, so no dragged width outlives its drag ([ADR 0080](0080-no-title-bar.md)'s last amendment).
- Not done: with the sidebar shown and a room under the open panes' least (Files alone in a window under about 750), the panes still lie over the whole chat area; and the side chat, Files and a third pane in a window under about 560 squeeze each under about 130, below what their rows hold. Tucking the sidebar whenever the panes would lie over the transcript would end the first, but would change windows up to about 1700 (a 1280 window with the side chat and Files would lose its sidebar), so it's the owner's to decide.

## Amendment (2026-11-19): a pane a file opened is kept too

The owner restarts Nushi for each update and lost their place: "when resetting Nushi for updates it doesn't open back up the file I had been looking at." A chat's file tabs are now kept across launches ([ADR 0070](0070-preview-tabs-are-the-panes-own.md)'s amendment of the same day), so the reason for `filesPassing` above, a kept pane coming back with none of its tabs, is gone, and it went with it: an open Files pane is kept however it opened, a file's opening or its own button, and comes back with its tabs. One rule, not two.
