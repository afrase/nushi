# 0026. The transcript lays out every item it has

## Context
Until milestone I the transcript was a lazy list. A lazy list only estimates its height from the items built so far, so the scrollbar thumb changed size while scrolling, the height shifted under the user, and following the bottom pulled a slow scroll back down. Laying out all of a long chat had its own cost: a 2,007-item chat took 39 frames and 4.2–4.7 s to lay out in a debug widget test, and its snapshot was 472 KiB.

## Decision
- The transcript is a `Column` in a `SingleChildScrollView`: every item it has is laid out. Each entry's widget is kept while its items are the same objects, so a streamed chunk rebuilds only the message it changes; a code block is highlighted once.
- It fills in from the bottom (2026-10-30): a transcript that opens at its bottom builds its last 12 entries in its first frame and 24 more in each frame after, until all are laid out; what shows stays put if the user scrolls up meanwhile. One that opens at its top (a finished subagent's) builds all at once.
- Following the bottom stops only on the user's own scroll up (not a fling's spring back past the bottom) and resumes at the bottom.
- **It has a long chat's recent part, and pages above it** (2026-11-04, as Claude's desktop app does). A chat opens with its last 200 items on the Mac (80 on the phone; `GetChatRequest.recent`, reaching back to a subagent's call so it shows whole, and to a permission prompt or question still waiting for an answer, the daemon sending a pending ask from above it with the part): about 18 turns of a long chat, several screens. It comes with the items before it whose subagent or background command still runs (`GetChatResponse.running`, kept up by their events), so the chat's background tasks list and stop before their page is in. Within a screen of the top the page before comes (`GetChatItems`, 200 more), a small spinner at the top meanwhile; a failed page says so with Try again and asks no more on its own. A page builds over a few frames as the chat fills in, and in the layout that adds what's above, the list moves down by its height (`BottomFollower.shift`, measured at the bottom of the first entry that was there): what shows doesn't move, a frame later or ever, and the thumb shrinks and moves down. What goes to a message not loaded (a pin, ⌘F's Load all) takes the pages up to it, 500 items a call, and puts them in at once: no call of the daemon's own. The rewind list (Esc Esc) takes the pages into the list only, so the transcript doesn't lay out a whole chat for it. What they bring builds over frames as a page does, four times as much a frame, and the pin is gone to (the find looks) once it's built. A chat left at its bottom goes back to its recent part, keeping the call its subagent or command pane shows, and all of it while a find looks; one left scrolled up keeps what it loaded, to open there again; one let go (not among the last six opened) loads again, as many items as it had if it was left scrolled up, so it opens there too.
- Why still no lazy list: what's loaded is laid out in full, so its height is exact and nothing estimated moves; pages keep how much that is small.
- The transcript's scrollbar runs the chat pane's whole height, beside the turn line and composer too, as Claude Code's does (`OverBottom`); the list itself ends above them, so a selection dragged onto the composer scrolls it and the selection's toolbar stays in sight, and the thumb maps its longer track in proportion. The phone's keeps its own (the composer a bar of its own, the bar showing only while it scrolls).

## Consequences
- Opening the 2,007-item chat (debug widget test): the first frame 260–300 ms before and after (filling in from the bottom bounded it already); all of what's loaded laid out in 0.52–0.63 s over 6 frames, from 4.2–4.7 s over 39; the snapshot 47 KiB. Release builds are several times faster.
- The scrollbar is over what's loaded, so it shrinks as pages come in. ⌘F finds in what's loaded and says so. What's worked out from the items (the Artifacts widget, the Files pane's marks) knows only what's loaded; Up takes the pages back to your last message when it isn't.
- A relayout costs what's loaded (300 items, debug: 28 ms per streamed chunk, 100 ms for a relayout; a lazy list was 8 ms per chunk but 418 ms to jump to the top). While it fills in (about 0.1 s), the thumb shrinks and the top isn't there yet.
- Going back to a lazy list brings the scroll bugs back unless the list can know its height.
