# 0112. Project tiles in the sidebar's list; a rail of them as an option

Status: accepted, 2026-11-12; amended the same day, after the owner tried v0.30.0-beta.1, and again after beta.2 (a tile's own image); amended 2026-11-13 (tiles move by dragging).

## Context
The redesign's mock (ADR 0111) has a Projects page: a narrow rail of square tiles beside the chats, one a project, and the same tile small before each project's name. ADR 0111 left it as a separate decision. The owner approved it (2026-11-12), with one complaint about the mock itself: same-shaped two-letter squares look alike, so a tile has to tell projects apart by more than two letters.

It was built whole and tried as v0.30.0-beta.1, the rail on at first. The owner, the same day: "I'm playing with the project view and I'm not really liking it. I like the icons on the full list though." Then: "Maybe have this project view be a config toggle", and "You can turn that side view off and on from the config."

## Decision
- **Every project has a tile, 20 square, before its name in its header in the sidebar's list.** This is the default, and all there is until the rail is turned on: the sidebar is otherwise as it was, the chats at its full width.
- **What a tile shows**, first of: an image of the user's own; a mark (an emoji or up to two characters); the repository's own icon; the folder's initials (the first letters of its first two words, or a word's first two) on a color. The first two, and one of the eight chat colors, are set in **Change tile…** (the project's header's menu), any without the others; the color fills behind a mark and behind an image where it's see-through. Colors are `Tokens.avatars`, as a chat's color is, kept by name, never a hex. A project with no chosen color takes the one its path names unless a project that joined the sidebar before it has it, then the next free: up to eight projects all differ, and a project's color changes only if an earlier one is removed.
- **A tile's own image** (the owner, after beta.2: "would be cool to set your own or have gif support", "We should support relatively high res ones and maybe just normal images"): **Choose image…** in Change tile… opens the system's panel. A PNG, JPEG, GIF or WebP by its first bytes, 16 MB at most, which the engine decodes; one that isn't is said in one line of the dialog, and what was there stays. Save keeps a copy in `tiles/` of Nushi's data folder under a random name, the file and the folder only the user's (0600, 0700), and the tile's entry of `projectTiles` names the copy (`image`), never a path into the user's folders. The copy is deleted when another replaces it, with **Remove image**, Reset and Remove from sidebar; Cancel keeps none. It's decoded at the size it's shown, in the screen's pixels, never its own, and drawn cropped to the square from its middle: a photo of any size costs a tile's worth of memory. The engine decodes an animated image's frames whole, so each is drawn again at the tile's size and the whole one let go: at rest an animated one keeps only its first frame at the tile's size too, and one larger than 4K (3840 × 2160 pixels, by its header) is refused.
- **An animated image plays only under the pointer.** At rest a GIF or an animated WebP shows its first frame; while the pointer is on the tile it plays, looping, at the file's own pace, and leaving it shows the first frame again. Never under Reduce Motion, and not while Nushi isn't in front (`appInFront`, the pulse clock's notion, ADR 0102). An image that played always would keep the window producing frames for as long as it showed: every frame draws the whole window, which is what ADR 0102 took the loops off the display's rate for. So at rest nothing of it exists but its first frame: no timer, ticker, listener or codec (`idle_frames_test` holds an idle window with one at no frames, and to the image's own frames while it plays), and the file is opened again each time it plays. The tile is a layer of its own.
- **A mark is centred by its ink.** A line of text is centred by its line box, which has the text's font's height and baseline whatever font a glyph comes from: capitals sat a little low, and an emoji, from the system's emoji font, visibly off the middle (the owner: "emoji are not centered"). No font's measures say where a glyph's ink is, so each mark is drawn once off screen at the screen's scale, in the style it's shown in, the box of what it painted found, and the text moved by the difference (`TileMark`); kept by text and size.
- **The repository's icon comes from the daemon** (`GetProjectIcon`, API 116; `internal/projecticon`): the app never reads a project's files. Only these paths, in this order, each as `.png` then `.jpg`: `icon`, `logo`, `.github/icon`, `.github/logo`, `assets/icon`, `assets/logo`, `docs/images/logo`; then the PNG with the most pixels in `Assets.xcassets/AppIcon.appiconset`, `macos/Runner/…` or `app/macos/Runner/…` of the same; then `build/icon`, `src-tauri/icons/icon`, `public/icon`, `public/logo`, `public/favicon`, `static/favicon`, `web/favicon`, `app/web/favicon`. A file is taken only if it's a regular file inside the folder (no link followed), 256 KiB at most, a PNG or JPEG by its first bytes, 32 to 1,024 pixels a side and no more than half again as wide as high; else the next path is tried. The app asks once per folder and keeps the answer until it quits. The limits are in `docs/security.md`.
- **Add project…** is a row of the Arrange menu, under New section: it adds a folder picked in the system's panel to the sidebar's projects (`keepProject`) and starts no chat. Under another grouping it's off, as an empty project shows only among the projects.
- **The project rail is an option, off at first**: Settings › Appearance › **Project rail** (`projectRail`, kept as `projectRailOn`). The beta saved `projectRail: true` for everyone, touched or not, so that key is ignored and dropped by the next save: who had the beta gets the new default.
- **With it on**, a column 48 wide stands at the left of the sidebar's chats, under its top strip: **All projects**, a short hairline, a 38-square tile for each project in the sidebar's order (kept empty ones too), then a dashed **Add project…**; the first is level with New chat, and it scrolls when they don't fit. A project's tile narrows the sidebar to that project (`sidebarProject`): its group, its pinned chats, its archived ones, and of each section the chats that are its, a section with none of them and "No folder" left out; ⌘1–⌘9 and ⌘[ ⌘] go through what shows. The tile picked wears a ring of `accentInk`, 2 wide and 2 off the tile, and a tile counts its project's chats that need you in a badge. When a chat of another project opens or starts, the sidebar goes to that project, so the open chat's row is never left out. It shows only while the sidebar is arranged by project and at least 250 wide; else, and with the rail off, a stored pick narrows nothing.
- **A tile moves by dragging, and so does its project** (2026-11-13). The rail has no order of its own: a drop sets the sidebar's (`sidebarOrder`), as dragging a project's header does, so the rail and the list never disagree, and a folder the order doesn't name keeps its place among the others ("No folder" too). It slides as the list does (`SlideList`): the others move apart where the tile would land, the tile faded there and a lifted copy under the pointer, its middle there; and a project's tile grows in and folds away as its header does. All projects and Add project… stand above and below the projects' tiles and never move; a tile dragged over one lands at that end, never past it. The drag waits until the pointer has moved 4 points (`dragSlop`), and a click is still one within them (`Pressable.slop`): Flutter's own wait is a point for a mouse, and a trackpad's click can move more. Near the rail's top or bottom edge a drag scrolls it. No key moves a tile, as none moves a header. A rail tile's tooltip shows at its right, level with it (`besideTile`), not below where it covered the next tile (the owner's ask); at its left with no room to the right, below as any other with none either side. Other tooltips stay below.
- **The sidebar keeps its width.** The rail takes 54 of it from the chats: turning it on or off moves neither the sidebar's edge nor the buttons in its strip (ADR 0111: a toggle never moves when pressed).

## Consequences
- A project's header is as high as it was; its name starts 26 further in, after the tile. "No chats" starts where the tile does.
- A daemon older than API 116 answers no icon; tiles show initials until it's replaced, and the app asks again once it's back.
- A tile's image is the one thing the app reads from a place the user picked, and keeps a copy of (`docs/security.md`). A copy whose `app.json` entry is lost stays in `tiles/` until removed by hand; nothing sweeps them.
- A played image is read and decoded again on each hover, a moment's work for a large file. Its frames are decoded whole before they're scaled, one at a time: while it's decoded or plays, a 4K one holds about 33 MB a frame (and the codec its own), gone once it stops.
- A golden draws a mark centred by its line: the measuring waits on the engine, which a test's clock doesn't (`centreMarksByInk`).
- No SVG and no ICO: the app draws neither without a new dependency, and an SVG is a document someone else wrote. A repository with only a `favicon.ico` or a `logo.svg` gets initials.
- The daemon doesn't know the sidebar's projects (the app keeps them), so it can't check the folder against them: the call is the Mac's only (`macOnly`, and the handler checks for the Mac's socket), as the calls that read any folder's git state are.
- With the rail on at the sidebar's first width (260), the chats' rows are 54 narrower.
- The phone has no tiles and no rail: its list is one column of every project.
- The headers of the list still start their drag after a point: only the rail's tiles wait for 4.

## Alternatives considered
- **The rail on at first** (this page until the owner tried it): the owner didn't like the view, and liked the tiles in the list.
- **The rail removed**: the owner asked for it as a switch instead.
- **Its switch in the Arrange menu** (the beta's "Project rail" row): the owner wanted it in Settings, and one place for it is enough.
- An animated tile that always plays, or plays while its project has a working chat: frames for as long as it shows, for decoration.
- Flutter's own `Image` for an animated file: it animates whenever it's on screen, and stops only by being taken out of the tree.
- Keeping the picked file's path: the tile would break when the file moved, and Nushi would read from the user's folders at every launch.
- Centring a mark by its font's ascent and descent: tried, and an emoji still sat a point or two off, as its ink isn't in the middle of them.
- The header's tile at 18, as the mock: at 20 its letters are a point larger and read better, and the header is no higher.
- A tile dropped over All projects or Add project… going back where it was: a drag that overshoots the first or last tile by a few points would be lost.
- An order of the rail's own: the rail and the list would disagree, and a project would have two places to keep.
- Widening the sidebar by the rail's width: its edge, and Arrange and the hide button with it, would move each time the rail came or went.
- Keeping the rail at any width: under 250 the rows beside it are too narrow to read a title.
- A color from the path alone: with five projects two often shared one. In the list's order rather than the order joined: dragging a project would recolor others.
- Asking the daemon for the kept projects' icons only: it would need the app's list sent to it first, for a call the Mac alone can make anyway.
