# 0080. No title bar

## Context
The owner: the bar with the traffic lights and the word Nushi is wasted space. macOS lets a window's content run under a transparent title bar.

## Decision
- The Mac window has no title bar: its content reaches the top, the traffic lights over an empty header at the top of the sidebar, as high as the chat's. Its hairline runs on into the chat's, Jira's, GitHub's and Settings' headers (`PaneHeader`), so the top of the window reads as one band.
- The bars that come and go at the top (the daemon's banner, its update, removed chats, notices) sit beside the sidebar, not across the window, or the traffic lights would cover them.
- The empty parts of those headers and of a pane's do what a title bar did, through `nushi/window`: a drag moves the window, a double click zooms it (`WindowDrag`). It sits under what's in a header, so a button there keeps its clicks.
- Amended by [0093](0093-nushi-places-its-own-window.md): the double click acts only when macOS counted the release as a second click (its interval and distance; a third does nothing), and does what the system's title-bar setting says (zoom, minimize or nothing); the zoom stays on the window's screen.
- Amended 2026-11-05 (the owner: double clicking some icons and places zoomed the window, "kind of annoying"): a double click zooms only in a header's truly empty space, not on what's in it (the agent's mark, the status, the model, a pane's title or its file's icon, a tooltip's icon, a disabled button) nor beside it: the bar's height through, and within 6 points across, so a gap inside a group and a click just above a word are the word's. A drag moves the window from all of that too, as from the empty space. A control, which `WindowDrag` tells by the pointer it shows (the hand, the text's I-beam), keeps its clicks and drags. `WindowDrag` is the header's Stack, so this is one rule for every header (`ChatHeader`, `PaneHeader`: the sidebar's top, Jira's, GitHub's, the side panes'). A native title bar zooms on a double click on its title too; Nushi's doesn't, as asked.
- Amended 2026-11-05 again (the owner, on 0.11.4: clicking a header's buttons and tabs shrank the window): AppKit still took the top 32 points, its title bar's height, for its title bar wherever no view there says it is content, and Flutter's view says nothing. Its theme frame's double-click recognizer zoomed the window on a double click on anything drawn there (a zoomed window shrank to its frame from before), and the window server dragged the window from it. A view over the content (`TitleBarCover`) now answers AppKit's own question, the one `NSButton` answers (`_opaqueRectForWindowMoveWhenInTitlebar`, as Chromium's tab strip does), with all of itself, and takes no event: the headers alone do the title bar's work. The question is AppKit's private one; `make test-window` asks AppKit, in a window it never shows, and fails if it stops asking. What covers the window with no header (the daemon's start and its update, Settings' dim) has a band of its own across the window's top, a header's height (`TitleBand`), that moves and zooms the window as the strip did; a click on the dim's band still shuts Settings.
- Moving the window takes the mouse's release from Flutter, which would go on dragging. The window tells it of a release it missed: once the button is up after a move, and whenever the pointer moves with no button held, or the next press comes, after a press it saw no release for.
- Amended 2026-11-05 a third time (the owner, on 0.12.1: the window sometimes stuck to the mouse and followed it after the button was let go, and Flutter then acted as if the button were still held): the header's pan asked for the drag over the channel, which comes late, and the window started it when the call came, from whatever event was current then. A drag that reaches the window server after the release has no release to end it. And `performDrag` returns at once while the window server runs the drag, whose release may never reach the app (Apple's documentation says so), so the window told Flutter of the release before the button came up. Now the header only arms a press on its drag area as it goes down (`arm`, with the press's time), and the window's press monitor starts the drag itself, from that press's own dragged event as it handles it, in the events' order, once the press is past Flutter's pan slop for a mouse (2 points; a double click's second press past a click's, 18) and only while the button is held now; an arm that comes after its press was let go, or for an earlier press, moves nothing. Flutter gets the press's release once, when the button is up: the real one if it comes, else one from the window (a watch on the button while the window server drags, or the next press), and a real one after that is dropped. The rules are `HeaderDrag`, plain state tested by `make test-window`. The pan stays in Flutter's arena, so a press that moves is no click of what's in the bar. The drag starts at the press's first move past the slop, not at its down as Electron's draggable regions do: a drag begun at the down takes every click's release, and the header's double click and the title's rename would never see one. What remains is the moment between the monitor's look at the button and the window server taking the drag, which no app-side call can close.

## Consequences
- The sidebar loses 16 points to its header. A chat gains 28; Jira, GitHub and Settings gain 18, their headers growing from 34 to 44.
- The traffic lights sit a little above the header's middle: they stay where macOS puts them, since moving them means re-placing them on every resize.
- A bar over the main area moves that area's hairline down, out of line with the sidebar's until it goes.
- In full screen the sidebar's header stays, with no traffic lights in it.
- The sidebar can be hidden (⌘\, `sidebarHidden`), and then nothing stands between the main area and the traffic lights. Every top header starts past them (`lightsInset`, 84 points), with the button that shows the sidebar there, and the bars, which then run across the window, start their text past them too. In full screen those insets stay, with no lights beside them.
- A bar at the window's top doesn't move the window while the sidebar is hidden; the header under it does.

**Amendment (2026-11-11):** the top is no longer one band with a hairline across the window. The sidebar sits on the window's background under a strip 32 high, level with the traffic lights, and the main area is a pane 8 in from the window's edges whose header is 56 high; the 8 points above the pane move and zoom the window too (`WindowEdge`), and `TitleBand` is that much higher. With the sidebar hidden a header still starts past the lights, which now sit in the pane's top left corner ([ADR 0111](0111-surfaces-and-floating-panes.md)).
