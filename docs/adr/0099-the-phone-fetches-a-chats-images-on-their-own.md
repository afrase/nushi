# 0099. The phone fetches a chat's images on their own

Status: accepted, 2026-11-07. Amended 2026-11-09: the Mac asks for references too. A chat of 200 screenshots (270 KB each) held 51.5 MB in its items, a copy of each to show (51.5 MB more), and all 200 decoded at once (281 MB), since the transcript builds every row; a chat left for another kept its items and copies. Now the Mac fetches each as it comes near, keeps 32 MB by hash (the daemon is on the same machine, so a fetch again is cheap), and a row more than two screens off lets its image go in its place (the phone's rows hold theirs: a fetch again goes over the relay); its image cache keeps 64 MB of decoded pixels, not Flutter's 100. Scrolled through, the same chat holds 12 images in its rows, 64 MB decoded and 32 MB of bytes.

## Context
The owner's phone sat on "Couldn't load it yet: trying again" for minutes. The chat's recent 80 items held four screenshots inline (2.5–3.4 MB each, about 12 MB): over a weak cell link and the relay (8 MB at once, then 1 MB/s) the snapshot outlived the phone's link checks (`app/lib/phone/link.dart`), the link dropped, and the load started over, four times in two minutes. Text loads in a fraction of that; images were the weight.

## Decision
- **References on request**: `GetChatRequest`, `GetChatItemsRequest` and `EventsRequest` take `image_refs`. With it, an image over 64 KB comes without its bytes: `ImageData.sha256` (hex of the bytes, as the transcript copy names its files), `size`, and `width`/`height` when the daemon reads them from the header (PNG, JPEG, GIF; else 0). Smaller ones stay inline: a call of their own would cost more. Without the flag nothing changes, so the Mac and older phones get what they did.
- **`GetChatImage{chat_id, sha256}`**: the bytes of one of that chat's own items' images whose hash matches, else NotFound; anything but 64 lowercase hex digits is refused before a lookup, so no path is ever made from it; at most 32 MB. Not macOnly: it's for the phone.
- **The phone asks for references** on all three (the Mac too, since the amendment above). An image arriving as a reference is fetched once it is within a screen of the transcript's view (the transcript lays out every row, so being built isn't enough), three at a time; one still waiting for its turn when it has scrolled away is dropped. Fetched images are kept in memory by hash (64 MB, least recently shown out first); a row that shows one holds it while it's there. An image the phone just sent to an idle chat is kept from the bytes it sent when its echo comes (matched by place and length), not fetched back. One it queues to a busy chat (or sends to a sleeping one, or for a subagent) has no message on its way to match: each over 64 KB is kept as it's sent, by the hash the phone computes of its bytes (a slice at a time, between frames), so the reference the daemon names it by when the message runs finds it; never by a hash the phone didn't compute. These count toward the same 64 MB, and one taken out of the queue to edit or remove is let go once no other queued message holds it. While it comes it shows a box of its size with a spinner; a failure says so with Try again. Try again on a failed turn fetches the message's images before sending them back.
- An older daemon ignores the flag and sends images inline, which show as before; the phone calls `GetChatImage` only for an image that came as a reference, so it never asks a daemon that lacks it.

## Consequences
- A chat of screenshots opens on the phone as fast as one without; each image then takes its own few seconds, and a dropped link costs one image, not the load.
- Hashing: an image is hashed once, as it comes in (from the agent, from a prompt) or read from its transcript copy's file name, and keeps its hash beside its bytes (`model.Image.Hash`, not stored); references and `GetChatImage` only compare hashes, so a fetch doesn't read the chat's other images. The phone hashes each large image it queues, and again one it takes back out of the queue.
- The queue's and a rewound message's images still come inline (a message being written, small and rare).

## Alternatives considered
- A smaller `recent` on the phone: still 12 MB whenever four screenshots are near the end.
- Longer link timeouts: the load still starts over on every drop, and the phone waits on bytes it may never scroll to.
- Thumbnails made by the daemon: an image library and its CPU for each image, when the phone shows them at 240 points anyway and a whole image is needed for the preview.
